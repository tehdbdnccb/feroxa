# Kisumu pilot playbook

## Pilot objective

Validate the operating loop rather than downloads:

`request → verified technician → quote → accepted repair → inspection → payment → handover → review`

## Supply cohort

Start with approximately 10–15 technicians/service providers. The first cohort should be manually curated and verified. Mix mobile technicians with workshop capacity.

## Initial service surface

- screen repair
- battery replacement
- charging problems
- camera
- speaker/microphone
- liquid-damage triage
- no-power diagnostics
- software/diagnostics

Keep board-level/microsoldering jobs as a specialist escalation path until evidence supports broader routing.

## Pilot geography

Start around central Kisumu and expand the service radius only when response time and repair quality remain reliable.

## First demand channels

- university/student networks
- premium-device owner communities
- independent repair shops that want incremental demand
- SME/NGO/business device administrators
- referrals from existing repair providers

## Operating metrics

Track per repair:

- time from request to first eligible technician
- claim-to-arrival time
- quote acceptance rate
- completion rate
- first-time-fix rate
- warranty claim rate
- dispute rate
- average repair value
- platform contribution per repair
- technician acceptance rate
- customer referral/repeat rate

Do not optimize for app downloads until the repair loop is reliable.

## Trust rules

- Never market an unverified technician as Apple-certified.
- Customer approval is required before chargeable repair work begins.
- Pre- and post-repair inspections should be mandatory for every completed repair.
- Evidence should be linked to the repair record.
- Customer payment must reconcile before handover confirmation.
- Disputes should freeze payout release until reviewed where appropriate.

## Expansion gate

Expand beyond central Kisumu only after the team has enough real jobs to understand quality, unit economics and technician liquidity. Then add Mombasa/Nairobi or a second African city based on measured demand and supply density.
